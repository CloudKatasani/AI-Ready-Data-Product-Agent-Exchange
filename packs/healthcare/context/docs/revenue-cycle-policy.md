# Revenue Cycle and Denials Management Policy

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy defines how Crestview measures and manages the revenue cycle from claim submission to payment: clean
claims, initial denials, net collections and days in accounts receivable. It applies to hospital and professional
claims and to the Claims & Denials data product.

## 2. Claims

A **claim** is a bill submitted to a payer for services rendered to one patient. It carries chargemaster charges
and the **expected reimbursement** — the amount the payer contract says Crestview should be paid. Revenue cycle
measures use expected reimbursement, not charges, wherever money collected is compared with money owed.

## 3. Clean claims

A **clean claim** passes the claim scrubber with no edits (BR-HC-018). The clean claim rate is clean claims divided
by submitted claims. Target: at least 90%. Valley General Hospital's clean claim rate fell in the third quarter of
2026 after a chargemaster update; coding and charge capture are reviewing the affected edits.

## 4. Initial denials

An **initial denial** is a claim the payer rejects on first adjudication, whether or not the denial is later
overturned (BR-HC-017). Denials are coded by reason: authorization, eligibility, coding, medical necessity, timely
filing and duplicate claim. The initial denial rate is denied claims divided by submitted claims; denied charges
are the charges on those claims.

A payer whose initial denial rate exceeds **15% for a quarter** is escalated to managed care contracting for a joint
operating review (BR-HC-019). Summit Advantage Health Plan crossed this threshold in the third quarter of 2026,
driven by authorization denials after a prior-authorization rule change.

## 5. Net collection rate

The **net collection rate** is payments received within 60 days of submission divided by expected reimbursement.
Only claims submitted more than 60 days ago are included, so the collection window is complete (BR-HC-021).
Target: at least 90%.

## 6. Days in accounts receivable

**Days in A/R** is the expected-reimbursement-weighted number of days from submission to payment. Claims still
unpaid after 60 days count as 90 days outstanding (BR-HC-022). Like the net collection rate it is measured on
claims older than 60 days. Target: below 40 days.

## 7. Roles

Patient financial services owns follow-up and appeals; coding owns coding denials; patient access owns
authorization and eligibility denials. Write-offs above $10,000 require the revenue cycle director's approval.

## 8. Data use

Revenue cycle measures are reported by payer, financial class, denial reason, facility and market. Individual
patient account balances are handled only in the patient accounting system.
