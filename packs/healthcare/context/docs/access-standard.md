# Ambulatory Access and Scheduling Standard

## 1. Purpose

This standard sets how the Crestview medical group measures and manages access to appointments. It applies to every
specialty and clinic and to the certified Scheduling & Access data product.

## 2. Appointment outcomes

Every scheduled appointment ends in exactly one outcome:

- **Completed** — the patient arrived and was seen.
- **No-show** — the patient neither arrived nor cancelled.
- **Cancelled** — the patient or the clinic cancelled in advance.
- **Bumped** — the clinic moved the appointment (provider unavailable).

## 3. Measures

- **No-show rate** — no-shows divided by appointments that were due to be kept (completed plus no-shows).
  Cancelled and bumped appointments are excluded from the denominator (BR-HC-014). Target: below 10%.
- **Cancellation rate** — cancelled and bumped appointments as a share of all scheduled appointments.
- **Days to appointment** — days between booking and the appointment date, averaged over appointments.
- **New-patient lag** — days to appointment for new-patient visits only.
- **Completed visits** — appointments the patient attended.

## 4. Access standards

A new patient should be offered an appointment within **14 days for primary care** and **30 days for specialty
care** (BR-HC-015). Neurology and dermatology currently exceed the specialty standard for new patients and are on
an access improvement plan with additional advanced practice providers.

## 5. No-show management

Behavioral health has the highest no-show rate in the medical group. Clinics with a no-show rate above 15% use
two-way text reminders, same-week rebooking and open-access slots. Overbooking is permitted only with the medical
director's approval and never for new patients.

## 6. Data use

Access measures are reported by specialty, facility, market, visit type and provider id. Provider names and patient
details are not shown in analytical outputs; schedulers use the scheduling system for individual bookings.
