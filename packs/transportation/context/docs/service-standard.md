# Delivery Service Standard

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard defines how Meridian Freight Lines measures delivery service for every truckload it moves: on-time
delivery, dwell, detention and service exceptions. It applies to all loads tendered under dedicated, contract and
spot agreements, and it is the definition used in quarterly service reviews with shippers.

## 2. On-time delivery

A load is **on time** when it is delivered inside the appointment window agreed with the consignee. Any delivery
after the window closes is late, however small the delay — there is no grace period. Reporting a load as on time
because it arrived within an hour of the window is a common shortcut and overstates service.

**On-time delivery** is the share of delivered loads that were on time. It is reported quarterly by region (the
region of the origin terminal), origin terminal, shipper segment and service level.

## 3. Customer-caused delays

Some late deliveries are caused by the customer: freight not ready at pickup, an appointment refused or rescheduled
by the consignee, or a receiving dock closed on arrival. Dispatch codes the delay cause on every late load and
customer service confirms customer-caused codes with the shipper.

Customer-caused delays are **excluded** from on-time delivery unless a question explicitly asks to include them.
This is the contract definition in every shipper service schedule. Building-materials job sites in the Mountain
region refuse appointments often, so including customer-caused delays changes the regional ranking materially.

## 4. Dwell and detention

**Dwell time** is the hours a tractor spends at the origin between gate-in and gate-out. It is measured on live
loads only; drop-and-hook loads swap a pre-loaded trailer and have no live dwell.

Shippers get **two free hours** on live loads. Every hour beyond that is **detention**, billed at 65 dollars an hour
and reported as accessorial revenue. Long dwell is the leading cause of late deliveries: in 2026 the Northeast
cross-docks T-03 and T-07 dwell more than twice as long as the network and drive most of the Northeast's lateness.

## 5. Service exceptions

A **service exception** is logged for damage, refusal, misroute or shortage. Lateness alone is not an exception.
The **exception rate** is the share of loads with a logged exception.

## 6. Escalation

A terminal whose on-time delivery falls below 90% for a quarter, or whose live-load dwell averages more than three
hours, is escalated to the regional operations manager for a dock review.
