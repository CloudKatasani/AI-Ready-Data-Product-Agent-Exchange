# Fleet Operations Standard

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard defines how Meridian measures the productivity of its tractor fleet: asset utilisation, empty miles,
fuel economy and revenue per truck. Measures come from the telematics (ELD) record of every tractor-day.

## 2. Asset utilisation

**Asset utilisation** is revenue-earning hours as a share of the hours a tractor was available for dispatch. Days
a tractor spends in the shop for planned maintenance or repair are **excluded** unless a question explicitly asks to
include shop days — a tractor in the shop is not available, and counting its zero revenue hours understates how
well dispatch uses the tractors it has.

Flatbed tractors earn fewer of their available hours than vans and reefers because construction freight is
seasonal and lumpy. The utilisation target is 72% to 80% for the fleet.

## 3. Empty miles

**Empty miles** are miles driven without revenue freight between a delivery and the next pickup. Bobtail miles —
a tractor running without a trailer — count as empty miles. Empty miles are reported as a share of all miles driven.
The West region runs far more empty miles than the rest of the network because freight out of the ports is
imbalanced: plenty of loads in, too few loads out.

## 4. Fuel economy

**Fuel economy** is total miles divided by total gallons for the fleet or slice; it is never an average of tractor
miles per gallon. Newer tractors are more efficient, and reefer tractors burn more fuel because the refrigeration
unit draws from the tractor's tanks.

## 5. Revenue per truck per week

**Revenue per truck per week** divides linehaul revenue by the number of tractors with telematics activity in the
period and by the number of weeks in the period. It is the headline measure of tractor productivity.

## 6. Planned maintenance

Every tractor is scheduled for preventive maintenance every 25,000 miles. Shop days are recorded in telematics so
utilisation can exclude them; unplanned breakdowns are recorded the same way.
