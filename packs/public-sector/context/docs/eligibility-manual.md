# Program Eligibility Rules Manual — Processing and Case Management

## 1. Purpose and scope

This manual sets out how Westland County Services receives, verifies and decides applications for its five
benefit programs — Food Assistance, Cash Assistance, Medical Assistance, Child Care Subsidy and Energy
Assistance — and how processing time, timeliness, backlog and caseload are measured for state and federal
reporting. Every timeliness figure given to the state or the Board of Supervisors must be calculated as described
here, from the certified Benefits Case Processing data product.

## 2. Receiving an application

Applications may be submitted online, in person at any of the eight service offices, by phone or by mail. The
date of receipt is the date the county first receives a signed application with a name and address. Online
submission is encouraged; the online application share is tracked as the digital self-service rate.

## 3. Complete applications and the processing clock

An application is **complete** when every required verification — identity, income, residency and household
composition — has been received. The processing clock starts on the complete date, not the receipt date.

**Processing days** count county business days from the complete date to the eligibility decision. Weekends and
county holidays do not count. The county processing standard is **30 business days** from a complete
application; expedited food assistance must be decided within 7 days.

## 4. Applications on hold for documents

When a caseworker needs a further document from the applicant — for example a new pay stub after a change of
employer — the application is placed **on hold for documents**. Days on hold are outside the county's control.

For that reason processing time and the timely processing rate are reported **excluding applications on hold for
documents**, unless a report explicitly asks to include them. The hold population is reported separately, so a
spike in holds is visible rather than hidden. In mid-2026 the North district introduced a new income-verification
checklist and placed far more applications on hold; including those applications makes North look slowest, while
the standard measure shows Riverside — short of eligibility workers — as the district falling behind.

## 5. Decisions and approval rate

Every application ends in one decision: approved, denied or withdrawn. The approval rate is approved applications
divided by approved plus denied; withdrawn applications are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator.
Eligibility decisions are made by a named caseworker and can be appealed; no automated system or agent decides an
individual application.

## 6. Timeliness reporting

The **timely processing rate** is decided applications with processing days of 30 or fewer, divided by decided
applications. **Applications over the standard** are decided applications whose processing days exceeded 30.
Both are reported monthly by program, office and district, and quarterly to the state.

## 7. Case backlog and backlog age

A case is open from enrolment until closure. The **case backlog** counts open cases with a new application,
renewal or reported change awaiting a caseworker decision at the end of the period. **Backlog age** is the mean
number of days those items have waited. Renewals are due every 12 months (6 months for Cash Assistance); a renewal
awaiting a decision counts toward the backlog.

## 8. Caseload

**Caseload** is open cases divided by filled caseworker positions; vacant positions are not counted. Office,
district and county caseloads each use the caseworkers at the matching scope. Offices whose caseload exceeds 130
open cases per caseworker are reviewed for temporary reassignment of work.

## 9. Records

Case files, eligibility determinations and notices are retained for seven years after case closure.
