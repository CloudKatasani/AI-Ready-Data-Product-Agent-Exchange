# Program Integrity and Payment Accuracy Procedures

## 1. Purpose

These procedures describe how Westland County measures benefit payment accuracy, records improper payments and
manages integrity flags, overpayments and recovery. They apply to the Benefit Payments product (certified) and the
Program Integrity product.

## 2. Payments

A **benefit payment** is an amount issued on a case for a benefit month by EBT, direct deposit or provider
disbursement. A payment issued in error and cancelled is **voided** and usually reissued. Payment metrics exclude
voided payments unless a report explicitly asks to include them.

## 3. Quality control and improper payments

A quality-control sample of payments is reviewed every month. An **improper payment** is any payment that should not
have been made or was made in an incorrect amount. Overpayments and underpayments both count.

- The **improper payment rate** is dollar-based: improper dollars divided by dollars issued.
- **Payment accuracy** is count-based: payments without an improper finding divided by payments issued.

The county targets payment accuracy of at least 96% and an improper payment rate below 3%. Errors are classified as
income verification, household composition, administrative error or duplicate issuance. In the second quarter of
2026 Cash Assistance income-verification errors rose sharply after wage-match updates were missed; a corrective
action plan is in place.

## 4. Integrity flags

Data matches — unreported income, employment data, address outside the county, duplicate participation and identity
verification — raise an **integrity flag** on a paying case. A flagged case is placed in open review and may be
referred for investigation. A case is **flagged for review** while its flag status is Open review or Referred.

An integrity flag is a review, **not a finding of fraud**. Staff and agents never label an individual as committing
fraud; only an investigator's decision, subject to appeal, can establish an intentional program violation.

## 5. Overpayments and recovery

When a review confirms that a recipient was overpaid, an **overpayment is established** and becomes recoverable by
repayment, benefit offset or state tax intercept. The **recovery rate** is dollars recovered divided by
overpayments established on payments issued in the period.

## 6. Retention

Payment and quality-control records are retained for seven years after case closure.
