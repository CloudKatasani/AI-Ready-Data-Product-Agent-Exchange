# Street Maintenance Service Standard

## 1. Purpose

This standard sets the repair targets Public Works commits to for county-maintained roads and how street
maintenance performance is measured from work orders.

## 2. Work orders

Every defect reported through 311 or found by an inspector becomes a **work order** against a road segment. A work
order is completed when the crew closes it in the maintenance system.

## 3. Repair targets

| Work type | Target from report |
|---|---|
| Pothole repair | 3 days |
| Sign replacement | 5 days |
| Streetlight repair | 7 days |
| Storm drain cleaning | 10 days |
| Crack seal | 14 days |
| Sidewalk repair | 30 days |

The **on-time repair rate** is completed work orders finished within target divided by completed work orders. Open
work orders at the reporting date form the **work order backlog**.

Pothole repair time reported to residents is measured on the 311 request — from the report to closure — so residents
see the same figure on the 311 app.

## 4. Pavement condition

Every segment is surveyed on a three-year cycle and scored on the **pavement condition index (PCI)** from 0 to 100:
below 40 is Poor, 40 to 69 Fair, 70 and above Good. Riverside roads score lowest after several years of deferred
resurfacing, and Riverside pothole repairs fell behind in spring 2026 when one patching crew was short.

## 5. Crews

Work is carried out by county crews or contractors on the street maintenance framework. Crew lead names are
personal data and are masked in analytical products.
