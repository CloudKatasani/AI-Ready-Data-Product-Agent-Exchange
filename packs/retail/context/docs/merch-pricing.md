# Merchandising and Price Execution Policy

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy covers the item master, initial margin standards, private label strategy, promotions and the
execution of price changes in stores. It applies to every buyer, merchandise planner and store team.

## 2. Initial margin

**Initial margin** is the margin built into an item's ticket price before any markdown: ticket price less cost,
as a share of ticket price. Items are banded Low (below 35%), Medium (35% to 49%) and High (50% and above).
Low-margin items in Active status are challenged at the quarterly line review; a category whose average initial
margin falls below 40% needs a recovery plan signed off by the merchandise director.

## 3. Private label

Private label items are sold under Harbor & Pine's own brands. They typically carry 6–10 points more initial
margin than equivalent national brands. The 2026 ambition is for private label to reach 35% of the assortment
and 30% of net sales.

## 4. Promotions

Promotions are planned in the promotion calendar by category with a mechanic (percent off, buy one get one,
bundle or clearance) and dates. **Promotional sales share** is net sales sold below ticket price as a share of
net sales. A category above 30% promotional share triggers a promotion effectiveness review.

## 5. Price changes

A **price change** is an instruction to change a shelf price in a store from an effective date: a markdown, a
promotional price or a permanent change. Store teams must re-ticket the shelf on or before the effective date.
A change counts as **compliant** only when executed on or before its effective date. Changes not executed by
their effective date are **overdue**; the shelf and the till disagree until they are fixed.

## 6. Escalation

Stores with more than 50 overdue price changes are escalated to the regional operations manager and receive a
pricing accuracy audit within seven days. Overdue price changes are the single largest cause of pricing
complaints and weights-and-measures findings.

## 7. Markdowns

Markdown depth is the percentage reduction on markdown price changes. Average markdown depth above 35% in a
category signals over-buying and is reviewed with the planner at the next open-to-buy meeting.
