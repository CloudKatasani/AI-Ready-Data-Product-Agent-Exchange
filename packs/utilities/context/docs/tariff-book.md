# Tariff Book 2026 — Summary of Rate Classes and Billing Rules

## 1. About this summary

This document summarises the rate classes in Northvale Energy's 2026 tariff book as approved by the Calloway
Public Utilities Commission. It is written for analysts and agents that report on bills and consumption. It
does not contain prices; the approved rate schedules remain the authority for charges. Where this summary and
the approved schedules differ, the approved schedules prevail.

## 2. Rate classes

Northvale serves four rate classes.

- **RS-1 Residential Service** applies to single-family and multi-family dwellings that take supply for
  domestic use. Energy is billed per kWh with a fixed monthly customer charge.
- **RS-TOU Residential Time-of-Use** is an optional residential rate with on-peak and off-peak energy prices.
  On-peak hours are 2 pm to 7 pm on weekdays, excluding holidays. Customers must have an AMI meter capable of
  15-minute intervals to enrol.
- **GS-1 General Service – Small** applies to non-residential customers whose monthly peak demand is below
  50 kW. Energy is billed per kWh with a fixed customer charge.
- **GS-2 General Service – Large** applies to non-residential customers at or above 50 kW of monthly peak
  demand. Bills include a demand charge per kW of the highest 15-minute demand in the billing period.

For reporting, **residential** means RS-1 and RS-TOU together; **small business** means GS-1; **commercial and
industrial** means GS-2.

## 3. Billing periods and statements

Customers are billed monthly on one of twenty billing cycles. Each statement covers one billing period and
shows the current charges, any past-due balance carried forward and the due date. The due date is 21 days
after the statement date. Late payment charges apply to balances unpaid after the due date.

Bills are normally rendered on actual reads from the AMI head-end. When a valid read is not available, the
billing system estimates consumption from the premise's history and flags the statement as **estimated**.
Estimated bills are trued up on the next actual read. The estimated bill rate is monitored monthly; a rate
above 4% triggers a review with metering operations.

## 4. Average bill reporting

The commission and the customer experience team track the **average monthly residential bill**. For this
measure:

- Only residential statements (RS-1 and RS-TOU) are included. Commercial bills are far larger and would
  distort the average.
- The average is the mean of current billed amounts per statement in the period.
- Past-due balances carried forward and one-off fees such as reconnection charges are excluded.

When a question asks for the "average bill" without naming a class, analysts and agents report the
residential average and say so. A question that explicitly asks about commercial customers, or all customers,
uses those classes instead.

## 5. Rate class changes

A customer may change between RS-1 and RS-TOU once in any twelve-month period. Non-residential customers are
reviewed annually and moved between GS-1 and GS-2 if their peak demand crosses the 50 kW threshold for three
consecutive months.

## 6. Low-income and medical programmes

Eligible residential customers may enrol in the bill assistance programme, which applies a monthly credit,
or register as medically dependent, which protects the account from disconnection during collections.
Enrolment status is sensitive and is not exposed in analytical data products.

## 7. Seasonal patterns

Residential consumption peaks in July and August with air conditioning load and again in January with
electric heating. Average bills follow consumption, so quarter-on-quarter comparisons should be read
alongside the same quarter of the previous year.
