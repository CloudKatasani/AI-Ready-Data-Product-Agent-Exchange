# Credit and Collections Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how Northvale Energy measures and manages receivables: how quickly customers pay, how
much is collected, when an account is in arrears and how payment arrangements are offered. It also defines
the receivables metrics used in financial reporting, so that days sales outstanding (DSO) and the collections
rate mean the same thing in every report, dashboard and agent answer.

## 2. Principles

Collections at Northvale are fair, explainable and decided by people. Automated scores may help prioritise
work, but every decision to escalate, arrange or refer an account is made and recorded by a named collections
specialist. Customers registered as medically dependent or enrolled in bill assistance are never placed on a
disconnection list. Analytical products and agents report receivables in aggregate only; they never recommend
action on an individual account.

## 3. The 60-day collection window

The **collection window** is 60 days from the statement date. A payment received within 60 days of the
statement date counts as collected for that statement; a payment received later counts as a late recovery.

The **collections rate** is the amount paid within 60 days divided by the amount billed, for statements whose
60-day window has closed. Statements issued in the last 60 days are excluded because their window is still
open; including them makes the collections rate look worse than it is. The same maturity rule applies to DSO.

## 4. Days sales outstanding (DSO)

Northvale uses the **payment-weighted method** for DSO. For each mature statement, the days to pay are the
days from the statement date to the first payment received within the 60-day window. Statements not paid
within the window are counted at 90 days. DSO is the billed-amount-weighted average of days to pay across the
statements in the period. This method rewards early payment of large balances and is stable month to month.

Finance previously used the countback method (month-end receivables over billed revenue, times 30). The
countback figure is still shown in the legacy monthly finance pack but is not the governed definition and
should not be compared directly with the payment-weighted DSO.

## 5. Arrears

An account is **in arrears** when a balance remains unpaid after the due date, which is 21 days after the
statement date. The arrears balance is the past-due amount carried forward on statements issued in the
period. Arrears thresholds are evaluated on the latest statement per customer.

| Days past due | Stage | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1–30 | Reminder | Automated reminder by the customer's preferred channel |
| 31–60 | Contact | Collections specialist contact; payment arrangement offered |
| 61–90 | Final notice | Written final notice; protected customers excluded |
| 90+ | Review | Specialist review before any referral |

## 6. Payment arrangements

Customers who cannot pay a past-due balance in full may agree a **payment arrangement** of up to twelve
monthly instalments on top of current charges. Arrangements are offered at the contact stage and may be
renegotiated once. A customer on an arrangement who keeps to it is not escalated. Collections specialists
record the reason for every arrangement and every refusal.

## 7. Customer contact data

Collections teams contact customers using the details in the customer master. These details are personal
information and are masked in analytical data products for anyone without PII clearance.

## 8. Reporting

The receivables dashboard reports billed revenue, arrears balance, collections rate and DSO monthly and
quarterly, by region and segment. These metrics fall under the receivables financial reporting controls; any
change to their definitions requires a new contract version approved by the product owner and Finance.
