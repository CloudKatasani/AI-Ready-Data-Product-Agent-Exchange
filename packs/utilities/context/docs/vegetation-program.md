# Vegetation Management Programme

## 1. Why vegetation matters

Tree contact is the single largest controllable cause of sustained outages on Northvale Energy's overhead
distribution network. Branches falling onto conductors during wind, snow or summer growth cause faults that
interrupt whole feeders, and tree contact outages tend to be longer than equipment failures because crews
must clear debris before restoring supply. The vegetation management programme exists to keep clearances
around overhead lines within standard so that tree contact outages, and the customer minutes they cause,
fall year on year.

## 2. Spans and inspections

The programme manages the network at **span** level: the section of overhead line between two structures.
Every span is inspected at least once per cycle by a qualified vegetation inspector, who records the species
nearest the conductor, an encroachment risk score from 0 to 100 and notes. The last trim date for each span is
taken from the contractor completion record and is a critical data element: the overdue calculation and the
annual commission report depend on it.

## 3. Trim cycles

Each span is assigned a trim cycle of **three, four or five years**:

- **3-year cycle** — fast-growing species (poplar, maple) close to primary conductors, and spans on feeders
  serving critical loads such as hospitals.
- **4-year cycle** — the default for most suburban distribution circuits.
- **5-year cycle** — slower-growing species and rural spans with wide rights of way.

A span is **overdue** when its last trim date plus its cycle is earlier than the reporting date. Trim cycle
compliance is the share of spans that are not overdue. The programme target is at least 92% compliance at
year end, and no feeder may have more than 15% of its spans overdue.

## 4. Clearance standard

After trimming, the minimum clearance is ten feet from primary conductors and five feet from secondary
conductors. Spans with clearance below six feet at inspection are classed as hot spots and prioritised
regardless of where they sit in the cycle.

## 5. Off-cycle and hot-spot trimming

Cycle trimming alone does not address feeders where risk has built up faster than planned. The programme
reserves up to 15% of the annual budget for **off-cycle trimming** on hot-spot feeders. A feeder qualifies when
two or more of the following are true in a rolling twelve months:

- tree contact outages are at least three times the network average for feeders of similar length;
- more than 20% of its spans are overdue;
- the average span risk score is above 60.

In the 2026 plan, feeder **F-2207** in the East region, fed from substation SUB-07, is the leading hot-spot
candidate: its spans are overdue and high risk, and it recorded a cluster of tree-contact outages in the third
quarter. Off-cycle work on F-2207 is scheduled for the fourth quarter and will be reviewed against its
customer minutes in the following quarter.

## 6. Prioritising the budget

Each quarter the reliability team ranks feeders by customer minutes interrupted from tree contact, excluding
major event days, and compares that ranking with trim cycle compliance and average risk. Budget is directed to
the feeders where trimming will avoid the most customer minutes, not simply to the feeders with the most spans.
The ranking is produced from the certified Distribution Reliability product and the Vegetation Risk product
once that product is certified; until then, vegetation figures are provisional and must be labelled as such.

## 7. Contractors and safety

Trimming is performed by approved vegetation services contractors under framework contracts. Contractors must
follow line clearance safety rules, and only qualified line clearance arborists may work within ten feet of
energised conductors. Completion records must be submitted within five working days of the work so that the
last trim date stays accurate.

## 8. Reporting

The programme reports quarterly to the Chief Operating Officer and annually to the Calloway Public Utilities
Commission. The annual report lists spans overdue by region, off-cycle work completed and the change in tree
contact outages on treated feeders.
