# Distribution Asset Management Strategy

## 1. Purpose

This strategy describes how Northvale Energy assesses the condition of its distribution assets, decides where
to maintain, refurbish or replace, and measures whether maintenance is being delivered as planned. It covers
transformers, poles, switches, reclosers, voltage regulators and capacitor banks on the distribution network.
Its aim is to direct investment to the assets whose failure would cost the most customer minutes, not simply to
the oldest assets.

## 2. The asset health index

Every asset carries an **asset health index** (health score) from 0 to 100, where 100 is as new. The score
combines age against expected life, inspection findings, test results (for example transformer oil analysis),
failure and repair history and loading. Scores are recalculated monthly by the asset analytics team from the
enterprise asset management system. The health score is a critical data element: condition bands, the
poor-condition share and replacement priorities all depend on it.

The health index indicates relative risk. It is not a prediction that an asset will fail on a particular date,
and it must not be presented as one.

## 3. Condition bands

Assets are grouped into three condition bands:

| Band | Health score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Poor** | below 40 | Elevated failure risk; candidate for replacement or major refurbishment within the plan period |
| **Fair** | 40 to below 70 | Serviceable; increased inspection frequency and targeted maintenance |
| **Good** | 70 and above | Normal inspection and preventive maintenance cycle |

The **poor-condition share** is the percentage of assets in the Poor band. The 2026 target is below 12% for the
network and below 15% for any single asset class.

## 4. Criticality

Each asset is assigned a criticality of High, Medium or Low based on the customers and loads it supplies and the
consequence of failure. A Poor asset of High criticality is prioritised ahead of a Poor asset of Low
criticality. Criticality and condition together form the risk matrix used to rank the replacement programme.

## 5. Vintage

Assets are also grouped into vintage bands by install year: pre-1980, 1980–1999 and 2000 onwards. Pre-1980
assets on overhead feeders are over-represented in the Poor band and are the focus of the pole and transformer
replacement programmes. Vintage on its own is not a reason to replace an asset in Good condition.

## 6. Preventive maintenance and compliance

Preventive maintenance (PM) work orders are generated from the maintenance plan for each asset class. **PM
compliance** is the share of PM work orders due in the period that were completed on or before their due date.
The target is **at least 90%** PM compliance for every region. Corrective maintenance (CM) work orders are raised
after a defect or failure and are tracked separately.

The **maintenance backlog** is the number of open work orders whose due date has passed. Planners review the
overdue backlog weekly and escalate any High-criticality asset with overdue PM to the regional operations
manager.

## 7. Linking condition to reliability

Each quarter, the asset team compares poor-condition assets by feeder with customer minutes interrupted from
equipment failure on the same feeders, excluding major event days. Feeders with both a high poor-condition share
and high equipment-failure customer minutes are prioritised. In 2026, older assets on feeder F-2207 in the East
region are in poor condition and are reviewed alongside that feeder's vegetation work.

## 8. Roles

The Director of Asset Management owns this strategy. Maintenance planners own the work order plan. Planner and
crew lead names are personal information and are not shown in analytical outputs.
