# Credit Risk Policy and Credit Quality Reporting Standard

## 1. Purpose

This standard defines how Ridgeline Bank measures the credit quality of its loan portfolio: delinquency,
non-performing loans, net charge-offs and the allowance for credit losses. One set of definitions applies
to the board risk report, the regulatory call report, the ALCO pack and every dashboard and agent answer.

## 2. Portfolio segments

Loans are reported in six segments set by the credit policy: residential mortgage, home equity, auto,
personal, commercial real estate (CRE) and commercial & industrial (C&I). CRE and C&I form the commercial
book; the other four form the consumer book. Segment is assigned at origination and does not change.

## 3. Month-end snapshots

Credit quality is measured from month-end loan snapshots. Balance measures such as loans outstanding are
the **average of month-end balances** across the months in the reporting period; they are never summed
across months. Ratios are computed over all loan-months in the period.

## 4. Delinquency

Days past due (DPD) is the number of days the oldest unpaid contractual payment is overdue at month-end.
Loans are grouped into Current, 30-59, 60-89 and 90+ DPD buckets. The **30+ days past due rate** is
principal 30 or more days past due divided by total principal. Delinquency is always principal-weighted.

## 5. Non-performing loans

A loan is **non-performing** when it is 90 or more days past due, or when it has been placed on
non-accrual because full collection of principal and interest is doubtful. The **NPL ratio** is
non-performing principal divided by total principal.

## 6. Loans held for sale

Loans the bank intends to sell are classified **held for sale** and carried at the lower of cost or fair
value. Held-for-sale loans are excluded from loans outstanding, the NPL ratio, delinquency, net
charge-offs and allowance coverage unless a report explicitly asks to include them. A distressed pool
marked for sale can distort credit ratios for the market that originated it, which is why the exclusion
is the default.

## 7. Charge-offs and recoveries

Consumer loans are charged off at 120 days past due (180 for mortgages) or earlier on bankruptcy;
commercial loans are charged off when a loss is confirmed. The **net charge-off ratio** annualises
monthly charge-offs net of recoveries and divides by average loans.

## 8. Allowance for credit losses (CECL)

The allowance is estimated for the expected lifetime loss of each segment and approved monthly by the
allowance committee. **Allowance coverage** compares the allowance with non-performing loans. Coverage
below 60% is escalated to the committee.

## 9. Decisions

Credit decisions — approving, declining, pricing or modifying a loan — are made by credit officers under
delegated authority. Analytical products and agents report portfolio credit quality in aggregate; they
never recommend action on an individual borrower.
